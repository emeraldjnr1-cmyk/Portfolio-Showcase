02 · Your problem

Twenty-four tees can look like twenty-four brands.

"I prefer a clean, modern, and stylish look over complicated or crowded graphics. Designs must suit oversized and boxy T-shirts and be supplied as high-resolution, print-ready files with full commercial usage rights."

Your post, Behance
Pain 01

A set of good prints that is not a collection

You want "minimalist, trendy teenage-inspired, and universally appealing designs" in one range. Three moods in one collection is where most ranges come apart: each tee is fine alone, but side by side they share no type, no palette and no line style.

Cost today: a shop page that looks like a marketplace, and a customer who liked one tee and does not recognise the next.
Pain 02

"Minimal" that turns out crowded, or empty

You said designs "should avoid crowded or overly complex visuals". Minimal is the hardest style to get right. With no rule for how much goes on a tee, some designs fill up with extras and others look like a blank shirt with a word on it.

Cost today: rounds of revision on taste, with nothing written down to settle them.
Pain 03

Artwork sized for a regular tee

Your designs "must suit oversized and boxy T-shirts". That cut has a wider chest, dropped shoulders and a shorter body. A print sized for a regular fit sits too small and too high on it, and a left-chest mark drifts toward the armpit.

Cost today: a sample round spent finding the right size, or stock printed with the artwork in the wrong place.
Pain 04

Files and rights that do not hold up

You need "print-ready files with full commercial usage rights" and artwork that "must be original". A flat PNG, a font licensed for personal use only, or a graphic traced from stock all cause trouble after the tees are made.

Cost today: a printer who asks for the file to be redone, and a design you cannot fully call yours.

05 · How the work runs

Three milestones, about four weeks.

This is the plan for the recommended option. You see finished designs at the end of week one, and each drop has two rounds of changes before it is signed off.

01

Week 1 · direction and Drop 01

You send your logo, your blank's measurements, and any tees or brands you like or dislike. I set the rules and prove them on the first drop.

  • Direction board: typefaces, palette and line style, agreed in the first two days
  • Drop 01 · Clean type: 8 finished designs on the oversized tee template
  • Placement spec set against your blank's measurements
  • First draft of the collection guide, so the rules are written before the rest is drawn
02

Weeks 2 to 3 · Drops 02 and 03

With the rules approved, the larger part of the collection is drawn in two batches so you can steer between them.

  • Drop 02 · Small illustration: 8 designs at one line weight
  • Drop 03 · Lettering: 8 designs, arched, stacked or hand-drawn
  • Colourway variants for each design: tee colour and ink
  • Two rounds of changes per drop, tracked in one list
03

Week 4 · files, mockups and handover

Everything is finished to print standard and handed over in one organised folder.

  • Print-ready files for all 24 designs: vector and 300 dpi PNG, one layer per ink
  • Placement and print-size spec sheet for every design
  • Realistic mockups of every design on an oversized tee
  • One-page collection guide, the 3 bonus designs, and the written rights transfer
