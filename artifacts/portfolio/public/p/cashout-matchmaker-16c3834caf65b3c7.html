02 · Your problem

Listings were the wrong bottleneck.

"we are not focusing on getting listing to put in our platform but to go deep into the buyer / tenant requirements and get agents to respond to those demands"

Your words, 9 November

That decision follows from four things you and Lai found in the last week.

Pain 01 · Getting listings

Agents have no reason to list again

PropertyGuru and 99.co do not let agents export their listings, the agent you asked posts each listing twice already, and many agents hold fewer than five listings at a time. Asking them to list on a new site is one more chore with no buyer attached.

Costing youA slow climb to your first 1,000 listings, and thin search results while buyers are arriving from your marketing.
Pain 02 · Shallow demand

A buyer's real need leaves with them

Today the buyer chat answers the search in front of it. When nothing fits, the buyer's budget, areas, timing and must-haves are not kept as a need that anyone works on afterwards.

Costing youEvery search that finds nothing is a qualified lead that walks away.
Pain 03 · Idle supply

50,000 agents, and nothing to answer

Your agent database is the largest asset Cashout has, and there is nothing to send them that is worth replying to. A blast asking them to list is easy to ignore.

Costing youThe value of the database, and the risk of burning the new WhatsApp number on messages that get blocked.
Pain 04 · Tracking the deal

Deals that close outside Cashout are invisible

As you put it on 6 November: "hard to track transactions happened outside Cashout". If a buyer and an agent meet off the platform, you cannot show Cashout made the introduction.

Costing youCommission and referral fees you cannot claim, and no numbers to show what Cashout is worth.
03 · The fix

One fix for each pain.

Every part of the build exists to remove one of the four pains above.

#
The pain
What removes it
01
Getting listings
Supply answers a buyer, privately. An agent or owner replies to a buyer brief with a screenshot, a link or a few lines, and Cashout reads it. No public listing is needed, nothing is copied from PropertyGuru, and owners keep their unit private until a buyer fits.
02
Shallow demand
The buyer brief. Cashout's AI interviews the searcher the way the listing interview does for owners: budget and loan readiness, areas and MRT, size, move-in date, must-haves, deal-breakers. It becomes a brief that stays open and keeps working after they leave.
03
Idle supply
A real buyer, sent to the right agents. Agents who opted in get the brief on WhatsApp through ChatDaddy, without the buyer's name or number. That is a message worth answering, because it is their commission.
04
Tracking the deal
Every introduction goes through Cashout. The buyer sees the shortlist, says yes to the ones they want, and only then are the two sides connected, with the time, the brief and the agent recorded on your board.

06 · What gets built

Two phases, each useful on its own.

Phase one works on cashout.com.sg from the day it ships, with briefs sent through ChatDaddy by your team. Phase two makes the whole loop run itself on WhatsApp.

01

Phase one · Demand and supply capture

  • The buyer brief interview in the buyer chat, for buyers and tenants, with a clear brief at the end
  • Supply cards: agents and owners add a unit privately on the site from a screenshot, a link or pasted text
  • Matching: each brief against Cashout's listings and the supply cards, and each new unit against open briefs
  • The demand board in admin: briefs, supply, matches, with status and the date of each
  • Export of briefs in the format your team uses in ChatDaddy, so marketing can send them from day one
  • Starts with one segment, rentals unless you choose otherwise, so the loop is proven before it widens
02

Phase two · The two-way matchmaker on WhatsApp

  • Agent and owner opt-in over WhatsApp, with the opt-in recorded
  • Briefs sent automatically through ChatDaddy's API to agents who opted in and fit the area
  • Replies come back through ChatDaddy's webhook; Cashout's AI reads the screenshot, link or text and checks it against the brief
  • The searcher gets the shortlist on WhatsApp and replies with a number to ask for a viewing
  • Contact details are exchanged only on the searcher's yes, and every introduction is recorded on your board
  • Credits ready for pay per use: agents spend a credit to answer a brief once you start charging
  • Sending kept within Meta's daily limits for the number, and checked against the Do Not Call registry where it applies
07 · Said upfront

Five things I will not pretend about.

Said upfront 01

Agents opt in first

A first message asks each agent whether they want buyer briefs. Only those who say yes get them. That keeps Cashout within the Spam Control Act and the Do Not Call rules, and protects the WhatsApp number from being reported and limited.

Said upfront 02

Meta sets the pace

A new WhatsApp Business number can start a limited number of conversations a day, 250 at first, rising as the number keeps a good quality rating. Reaching 50,000 agents happens over weeks, not in one blast.

Said upfront 03

ChatDaddy on the business number

ChatDaddy needs to run on +65 8808 0669 through the official WhatsApp Business API, not on a personal phone, for sending at this volume. Your ChatDaddy plan and Meta's message fees are billed to you directly.

Said upfront 04

Built with Lai

Lai leads the code. The work is done on its own branch, Lai reviews it, and you both agree when it goes live.

Said upfront 05

No invented matches

A match is only sent to a searcher when the unit's facts fit the brief, read from what the agent or owner actually sent. If the facts are unclear, Cashout asks the agent instead of guessing.
