02 · Your problem

Fifteen episodes of action, before anything moves.

"Cinematic composition, expressive character performances, detailed environments, and sophisticated action choreography."

Your brief, Behance, November 2026
Pain 01

Action choreography is found while drawing keys

You named "dynamic combat choreography and cinematic direction" as the tone you admire. In a 2D pipeline the fight is usually worked out in thumbnails and then in key drawings. When a camera move or a beat changes late, the keys around it get drawn again.

Cost today: redrawn key frames every time a beat or a camera move changes, on the most skilled hours you are hiring.
Pain 02

A proof of concept is a lot of drawing for a small team

Your brief says "potentially a short animated proof of concept". Fully hand-animated, even a short scene needs layout, keys, in-betweens, colour and compositing before anyone outside the studio can see it move.

Cost today: nothing moving on screen to show partners while visual development is still being paid for.
Pain 03

Detailed environments, drawn again for every angle

"Detailed environments" and "scale" across three acts means the same places seen from many cameras. Drawn by hand, every new angle is a new layout, and the perspective has to stay consistent across artists and episodes.

Cost today: a new layout drawing for every new camera, and scale that drifts from shot to shot.
Pain 04

Several artists, no shared shot to work from

You are "open to working with individual artists or small animation teams". Freelancers in different places each read a still or a written brief their own way: where the camera is, how big the figure is, where the light comes from.

Cost today: briefing time and mismatched shots that have to be corrected after they are drawn.

05 · How the work runs

Three milestones a month, your vision leading.

01

Boards and blockout · week 1

I read the Season One Artist Bible and the script pages for one key scene you choose. Your references are tone only; the look we build is yours. Nothing is designed against your direction.

  • Thumbnail storyboard panels for the scene, with shot sizes and timing
  • Grey 3D blockout of the location with figures at the right scale
  • Shot list with lens and camera move per shot
  • Sign-off here before any choreography is animated
02

Camera and choreography · weeks 2 to 3

The fight or key action is blocked on rigged figures, shot by shot, and cut into a timed animatic. You and your artists comment on specific frames, and I re-time in the 3D scene.

  • Camera blocking with real lens choices and moves
  • Action choreography on rigged placeholder figures
  • Timed animatic at 24 fps with temp sound
  • Two rounds of shot notes
03

Look and handover · week 4

The locked scene is rendered with toon shading and a line pass, lit per act, and exported as a draw-over pack your 2D team can open in Clip Studio Paint, Toon Boom Harmony or TVPaint.

  • Cel-shaded render of the scene and a line pass
  • Environment and lighting mood pass for OMENS, EXODUS and SUN BLED
  • Draw-over pack per shot: layout frames, line frames, animatic, notes
  • All Blender files, cameras and lights, in your name
