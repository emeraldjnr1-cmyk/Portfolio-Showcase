02 · Your problem

A sketch is not yet a garment.

"...bring fresh concepts from initial sketches to production-ready garments."

Your post, Behance, November 2026
Pain 01

Sketches that stop at the sketch

You asked for "production-ready garments", not drawings. A sketch with no measurements, seam notes or trim list leaves your pattern makers to fill in the gaps themselves.

Cost today: extra sample rounds, each one using handloom cloth and your sample room's time.
Pain 02

Three teams, three sets of questions

You want a designer who can "work closely with our pattern makers, sourcing team, and merchandisers". Each team needs different facts about the same style, and a sketch answers none of them.

Cost today: the same questions asked three times, and styles waiting on answers.
Pain 03

Styles that do not add up to a collection

You asked for "cohesive collections that align with our brand identity". Pieces designed one at a time rarely share a palette, a border or a trim, so they look unrelated side by side.

Cost today: a rail that does not sell as a set, and a brand that reads differently every season.
Pain 04

Designs that ignore the loom

You named "commercial goals". Handloom cloth has its own rules: a set width, woven borders, a pallu. A design that ignores them wastes the most valuable part of the fabric.

Cost today: more metres per garment than needed, and borders cut away instead of used.

05 · How the work runs

Three milestones, about five weeks.

Each milestone ends with something you can open and approve. The timing below is for the recommended 12-piece collection. Sampling rounds follow your sample room's pace, since the samples are made in Hyderabad.

01

Week 1 · direction and range plan

I start from what you send: your brand notes, the fabrics available this season with their widths, your size run and one tech pack or spec sheet your team already uses, if there is one.

  • Story and moodboard for the collection, tied to Kaladhar's identity
  • Palette with lab-dip references for your sourcing team
  • 12-piece range plan: which garment, in which fabric, in which colourways
  • Concept sketches for all 12 pieces, for your approval before detail work
02

Weeks 2 to 3 · flats and tech packs

Once the direction is approved, every sketch is turned into the documents your pattern makers and sourcing team work from.

  • Front and back technical flats for all 12 styles, as editable vector files
  • Full tech pack per style: POM table, grading, construction, trims and fabric consumption
  • Border and motif placement marked on each flat
  • Reusable tech pack template set in your format, free on this option
03

Weeks 4 to 5 · line sheet and sampling reviews

While the first samples are being made, your merchandisers get their document. When samples are ready, the review rounds begin.

  • Line sheet for merchandisers: style codes, flats, fabrics, colourways and sizes
  • Remote sampling reviews: up to two rounds per style, from your photos and video
  • Numbered corrections with the tech pack updated after each round
  • Final file set: every flat, tech pack and the line sheet in one shared folder
