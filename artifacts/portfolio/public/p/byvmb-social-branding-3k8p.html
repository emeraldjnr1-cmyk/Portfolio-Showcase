02 · Your problem

The designer's own feed is the first thing to slip.

"A growing design-led brand designer who wants to build a strong, intentional visual presence online."

Your post, Behance, 25 November 2026
Pain 01

Your feed is judged like your client work

A founder looking for a brand designer opens your Instagram before anything else. You asked for an "intentional visual presence", and one rushed post with the wrong type or spacing reads as a lack of care, right next to work that took weeks.

Cost today: every off-system post is a prospect's first look at your standards.
Pain 02

Client deadlines win, so posting stops

You asked for someone "organised, consistent, and proactive" because the account runs on whatever time is left. When a client project lands, the feed goes quiet, and every restart begins from cold.

Cost today: quiet weeks, and a fresh push each time you come back.
Pain 03

Reels go out as filmed, or not at all

You want someone to "check, review, and refine reels before posting". Without that step, the hook lands too late, text sits under the Instagram buttons, captions are missing, and the cover frame breaks the grid.

Cost today: reels that lose people in the first seconds, and a grid that looks unplanned.
Pain 04

Nobody tracks what is working

"Track what's working and suggest ways to improve reach and engagement" is on your list because, today, the next idea is a guess. Time goes into formats that may not bring saves, shares, follows or enquiries.

Cost today: content hours spent without knowing which posts earn them back.

05 · How the work runs

The first 30, 60 and 90 days in the role.

30

Days 1 to 30 · build the system, start posting

Week one is setup and nothing is posted until you have approved it. From week two the account posts on the calendar.

  • Audit of your last 30 posts: what already looks like byVMB, what does not
  • Template kit in Figma and Canva: post, carousel, story, reel cover, highlight covers
  • Grid plan nine posts ahead and the first weekly approval board
  • Scheduling set up in Meta Business Suite, with access you grant and can remove
  • Baseline numbers recorded on day one, first what-worked report at day 30
60

Days 31 to 60 · full rhythm and reels

The full weekly rhythm runs, and every reel passes the 8-point check before it posts.

  • Three feed posts a week, stories on four days, two reels a week
  • Two format tests, for example case-study carousels against process reels
  • A running idea bank of at least 30 ideas, aligned to the brand
  • Month two report with the three changes applied to the next calendar
90

Days 61 to 90 · double down and hand over

More of what worked, less of what did not, and a system that keeps running whatever you decide next.

  • Calendar weighted toward the top formats from months one and two
  • Three-month report: what grew, what did not, what to keep
  • A playbook: templates, calendar, reel checklist and report template, in your name
  • A clear plan for the full-time scope, if you want to continue
