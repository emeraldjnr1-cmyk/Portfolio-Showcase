02 · Your problem

Eight kinds of artwork. One brand has to come out the other side.

"The illustrations should be suitable for use on children's clothing and align with the brand's intended audience."

Your brief, Behance, November 2026
Pain 01

Art that looks lovely on screen and prints badly on a tee

Hairline strokes, soft gradients and eight-colour palettes are normal in illustration portfolios. On a child's tee they turn into blurry edges, extra screens, and a printer who quotes you more per unit or asks for the file to be redone.

Cost today: rejected samples and a higher print cost on every unit, on a product where margin is already thin.
Pain 02

Eight deliverable types that stop looking like one brand

Logo, 20 designs, patterns, characters, icons, lettering, baskets and packaging. If the eye style, line weight or palette drifts between them, the shop reads as three brands, and a parent who loved one tee does not recognise the next one.

Cost today: lower repeat buys, because recognition is what brings a parent back for the next size up.
Pain 03

A scope this size stalls without an order

"Starting with 20 designs" only works if the logo, palette and core characters are locked first. Draw the tees before that and every brand change sends all 20 back to the desk.

Cost today: weeks of rework and extra revision rounds that nobody budgeted for.
Pain 04

"Editable source files" that arrive flattened

Many illustrators hand over a PNG and call it done. You then cannot resize the bear for a bib, recolour it for a winter drop, or hand a clean vector to a packaging supplier.

Cost today: paying again for every new product that needs the same character.

05 · How the work runs

Three milestones, in the only order that holds.

01

Foundation · weeks 1 to 2

We start with your brand name, your audience (age range, boy, girl or neutral, the price point) and three animals or characters you already love. From that I build the style sheet and the logo system.

  • Style sheet: shapes, eye style, line weight, colour rules, do and do not
  • Logo: primary, stacked, icon, one-colour, reversed, with clear-space and minimum-size rules
  • Colour palette with print references and a swatch file
  • Three hero characters, front view, in final colour
  • Sign-off here before any apparel is drawn
02

Apparel · weeks 3 to 4

The 20 designs come in two batches of ten so you can steer after the first. Each design sheet shows the artwork, the placement on the garment, the print size and the colour count.

  • 20 apparel designs mixing character art, lettering and small motif placements
  • Remaining characters, custom icons and lettering pieces
  • All-over repeat patterns and the collection spread
  • Simple mockups of every design on a flat garment
03

Packaging, baskets and handover · week 5

Packaging is drawn last because it reuses everything above. You get the full file set with a map, so a printer or packaging supplier can be sent one folder.

  • Box, hang tag, sticker sheet and thank-you card, with dielines where you give me supplier sizes
  • PR basket and gift basket designs
  • AI, SVG, PDF and 300 dpi PNG for everything, plus the swatch file and file map
  • A 20-minute written handover note on how to brief a printer with these files

06 · Why this is an easy yes

Low risk by design, not by promise.

The value maths
One design pays for itself on one product

A single tee design sold at a typical kids price only needs a handful of units to cover its share of the Standard option. The logo system and packaging are bundled, not billed separately.

What you keep
Everything, in your name

Layered source files, vectors, PNGs, the palette and the style sheet. Full rights to use them on products, packaging, ads and social. No licence fees later.

The stop rule
Milestone 1 is the proof

You see the logo system and three finished characters before any apparel is drawn. If the hand is not right for your brand, you stop there, keep the foundation work, and nothing else is owed.

Revisions
Two rounds per milestone, in writing

Clear enough that neither of us argues later. Anything that is my error gets fixed outside the count.

Launch bonus on the Standard option
Mockups of all 20 designs, not just the key ones

Your brief asks for simple mockups of key designs. Pick Standard and every one of the 20 gets a flat garment mockup, so you can put the whole first drop in front of a buyer or a supplier before a single unit is printed.
