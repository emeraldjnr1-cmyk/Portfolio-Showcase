02 · Your problem

Your furniture exists on paper before it exists in a photo.

"Taking 2D drawings from our manufacturer in China and converting them into photorealistic renderings for our marketing content and embed on our website."

Your job post, Cad Crowd, November 2026
Pain 01

A drawing does not sell a sofa

Your manufacturer sends line drawings. A customer on your website needs to see wood grain, fabric and proportion. Without a render, the only other route is shipping a sample and arranging a photoshoot for every product.

Cost today: products waiting to go live until an image exists for them.
Pain 02

A catalogue that looks stitched together

When each product is rendered with different light, camera height and background, the website gallery looks like five different shops. Furniture is bought on feel, and an uneven catalogue feels cheaper than the product is.

Cost today: a brand that looks less premium than the furniture it sells.
Pain 03

Fabric is where renders give themselves away

Hard wood and metal are forgiving. Upholstery is not: cushions that look like plastic, seams with no tension and no creases tell a buyer the image is fake. That is why your post asks for Marvelous Designer.

Cost today: buyers who do not trust the texture they are about to order.
Pain 04

Six months is a long time with the wrong person

An ongoing part-time role only works if the same person keeps the same standard, reports hours you can check, and does not need re-briefing every month.

Cost today: hours you cannot see and a look that drifts as people change.

05 · How the work runs

Week 1 sets the look. Every week after follows it.

01

Style template and first two products · week 1

You send two drawings and a few images of the look you like. I build the studio setup and the lifestyle room, then put both products through it so you approve the look on your real furniture.

  • Studio lighting, camera heights, lens and background fixed
  • One lifestyle room that suits your range
  • Two full product packs for your approval
02

Weekly batches · week 2 onward

You drop new drawings in a shared folder. Each product gets a quick clay check against the drawing before materials and final renders, so proportions are agreed before render time is spent.

  • Clay check within 1 working day of a complete drawing
  • Final pack within 3 working days
  • Batch tracker shared every Friday with hours from the timer
03

Monthly check-in · every 4 weeks

A short written review: hours per product against the cap, anything to adjust in the template, and which products would benefit from colour variants or a turntable.

  • Hours per product compared with the cap
  • Template tweaks agreed in writing
  • Model files and exports up to date in the shared folder
