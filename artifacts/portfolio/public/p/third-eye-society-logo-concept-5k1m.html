02 · Your problem

One parent mark, many product lines, no cliches.

"The brand is intended to bring multiple product lines under one distinctive parent identity, including mushroom-based products and other alternative compounds."

Your post, Behance, Logo Design | Third Eye Society
Pain 01

One mark has to hold every product line

A parent logo drawn for the mushroom line only will not fit the next line. A logo too neutral to fit them all says nothing. The parent identity needs a built-in way to carry lines that do not exist yet.

Cost today: every new line needs its own logo, and the shelf stops reading as one brand.
Pain 02

Third-eye imagery turns generic fast

You want it "artistic without looking generic or overly commercial". The eye in a triangle, the stock mandala and the dripping font have all been used many times. With 29 applicants on this post, several concepts will reach for them.

Cost today: a mark people have already seen, which is the opposite of "bold, distinctive, and memorable".
Pain 03

Psychedelic detail breaks at small sizes

You asked for "a distinctive symbol that can work independently as a brand mark". Vintage psychedelic art is dense by nature. On a jar lid, a sticker or a profile picture, fine detail fills in and the symbol becomes a smudge.

Cost today: a symbol that only works large, so it cannot stand alone where a brand mark is used most.
Pain 04

Choosing a designer from one concept

You are "only looking for an initial design concept". A single image shows a mood. It does not show whether the idea survives a light ground, a small size or a second product line, and that is what the paid work depends on.

Cost today: the risk of picking on mood and finding the limits after the work has started.

04 · The end result

The concept, presented in full.

Everything below is original vector work drawn for this proposal. It is an initial concept, as your post asks: the idea is fixed enough to judge and open enough to change with your feedback.

Creative approach

The third eye is usually drawn as an eye in a triangle. I turned it upright instead, the way it sits on the brow in older esoteric drawings, and placed it inside a sun: an eye that gives off light, not one that only receives it. That is the idea of expanded perception in one shape. The rays alternate between straight blades and flames, borrowed from tarot sun cards and 1960s underground handbills, and the whole mark is flat color with an offset second ink, like a two-color screen print. The lettering is fat, slab-heavy and arched over the mark like a poster headline, with "Society" curved back underneath so the two lines open like a pair of lids. Last, the pupil is left as a changeable part: the parent brand carries a spark, the mushroom line a cap, and each future line its own sign. One sun, many pupils.
