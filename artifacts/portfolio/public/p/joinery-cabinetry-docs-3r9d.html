02 · Your problem

Four sources, one factory, and no room for guesses.

"Particular attention is required for appliance integration, storage, hardware, stone, mirrors, material junctions and coordination with adjoining wall finishes."

Your job post, Cad Crowd, November 2026
Pain 01

Four sources that do not all say the same thing

The architectural drawings, the Revit model, the client markups and the updated FF&E schedule were each changed at different times. Until someone reconciles them, nobody can say which version of a cabinet is the real one.

Cost today: outstanding design and finish decisions that hold up every drawing that depends on them.
Pain 02

The details a factory cannot guess

An appliance cavity, a stone apron over a drawer, a mirror edge against tile, a tall unit meeting a painted bulkhead. If the drawing does not show it, the factory makes its own assumption.

Cost today: cabinets that arrive a few millimetres wrong, after they have crossed an ocean.
Pain 03

Shop drawings that come back with the factory's assumptions

A manufacturer in China redraws your set in its own format. Every gap in the documentation comes back as their interpretation, and someone has to catch it before production starts.

Cost today: your architect and builder left to review someone else's reading of the design.
Pain 04

An hourly job without fixed stage gates

You wrote it yourself: "We would agree on deliverables and approve each stage before work proceeds." Without agreed deliverables per stage, hourly work drifts and you only see the total at the end.

Cost today: no clear point where you can check quality and decide to continue.

03

Shop drawing review · when the factory issues them · about 16 hours

When the manufacturer's shop drawings arrive, I check them against the issued set and send marked-up comments within 5 working days.

  • Sheet by sheet mark-up of every difference from the set

  • One re-check of the factory's revised shop drawings
