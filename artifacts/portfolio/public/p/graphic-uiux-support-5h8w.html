02 · Your problem

Two kinds of design, an open scope, and no time to waste.

"Professional-quality work, fast turnaround, and reasonable cost."

Your brief, Behance, November 2026
Pain 01

The scope is not written down yet

The brief asks for "UI/UX design assets and graphic design materials aligned with the project". That is the right ask, but without a list of screens and pieces, every designer quotes a guess and the first week goes on emails about what is in and what is out.

Cost today: a week or more lost before the first screen, plus quotes you cannot compare.
Pain 02

Screens and graphics that do not look like one brand

Hiring a UI designer and a graphic designer separately is common. Each picks their own blues, fonts and spacing, so the app says one thing and the social posts and banners say another.

Cost today: two briefs, two sets of revisions, and a product that looks stitched together.
Pain 03

Small requests that wait days

A banner resize or a new button state is ten minutes of work, but it sits in a queue behind other clients. Meanwhile the developer, the launch post or the ad is blocked.

Cost today: days of waiting on ten-minute jobs, and the people waiting on them sit idle.
Pain 04

"Reasonable cost" with no ceiling

Open hourly work on an open scope means you only learn the total at the end. That makes it hard to plan the rest of the project budget around design.

Cost today: no fixed number to plan against, and a bill that grows with every change.

05 · How the work runs

Three milestones, one batch a week.

01

Scope and design system · week 1

Day 1 is discovery: your answers to the list at the bottom of this page become a one-page scope. The rest of the week builds the system everything else is made from.

  • One-page scope: every screen and graphic named and placed in a weekly batch
  • Design system: colour tokens, type scale, spacing, buttons, inputs, cards, tags
  • Two key screens in full, so you can judge the direction on real layouts
  • Friday batch 1, sign-off here before the rest is designed
02

Screens and graphics · week 2 to 3

The remaining screens and the graphic set come in two Friday batches, so you can steer after the first one. The 24-hour lane stays open for small requests the whole time.

  • The rest of the UI/UX screens, mobile and desktop where the scope needs both
  • Key states on each screen: empty, loading, error, success
  • Graphic set: social posts, stories, web banners, ad creatives, from the same system
  • 2 revision rounds per batch
03

Handover · end of week 3

Everything is packed so a developer, a marketer or another designer can pick it up without asking.

  • Editable source files in the design tool we agree on day 1
  • Exports: PNG, SVG and PDF at the sizes each platform needs
  • Short written guide to the system: which colour, size and button goes where
  • A file map so every asset is easy to find
