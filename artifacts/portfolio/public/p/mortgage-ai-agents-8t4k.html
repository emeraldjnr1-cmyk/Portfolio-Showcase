02 · Your problem

Every file needs 30 to 40 documents, and they never arrive in order.

"Hundreds of client documents arrive daily."

Your job post, Upwork, November 2026
Pain 01

Opening, renaming and filing by hand

Documents land in Gmail and Slack. Someone opens each one, works out what it is and whose file it belongs to, renames it to your convention and drags it into the right Zoho WorkDrive folder.

Cost today: staff hours every working day spent on filing instead of on clients and lenders.
Pain 02

Missing documents found late

With 30 to 40 documents per client file, a missing letter of employment or bank statement is easy to miss until a lender or underwriter asks for it.

Cost today: files that stall while you chase the client at the last minute.
Pain 03

An altered document looks normal on screen

An edited pay stub or bank statement does not announce itself. Checking every PDF for signs of editing by eye is slow and depends on who opened it.

Cost today: fraud risk on files you send to lenders under your name.
Pain 04

French documents need reading in English

Before anyone can review a French pay stub or letter, someone has to translate it, and the original still has to be kept as it came in.

Cost today: a wait on every French document before the file can move.
Pain 05

Nobody can see a file's status at a glance

Which documents came in today, which are still missing and which look wrong lives in people's heads and inboxes.

Cost today: time spent asking around before every client or lender update.
Pain 06

AI cannot become a privacy problem

These are IDs, pay stubs, T4s and bank statements of Canadian clients. Moving them through AI has to keep you in control of where they go.

Cost today: a reason to hold back on automation unless the data question is answered first.

05 · How the work runs

Four stages, tested on your real documents before launch.

n8n runs the pipeline on your server. AI is used only where reading a document needs it. Renaming, filing, the checklist and status updates are plain code, so they do the same thing every time. LangGraph comes in for later workflows where an agent has to reason across several steps, such as underwriting notes.

01

Discovery and mapping

Before any automation, I map how your files work today, so the agent files the way your team already does.

  • Your naming convention, written as rules
  • How a document is matched to its file number
  • Your WorkDrive folder tree per client file
  • The document checklist per deal type
02

Intake and reading

Every document enters one queue, whatever channel it came from, and is read once.

  • Gmail and Slack intake into one queue
  • Classification of each document type
  • Data extraction from IDs, pay stubs, letters of employment and bank statements
03

Checks

The parts that protect the file, with a person kept in the loop.

  • Completeness check against the checklist per client file, with deterministic rules, not AI
  • Alteration signals flagged for human review, never auto-rejected
  • French to English translation stored alongside the original, original never overwritten
04

Filing, status and go-live

Documents reach their folder and your team sees where every file stands.

  • Rename and file into WorkDrive by file number through the Zoho WorkDrive API
  • Slack status per file: received, missing, flagged
  • Review queue for anything low-confidence
  • Testing on a real sample of your documents before go-live, then handover docs

06 · Privacy and data handling

Your clients' data stays in your systems.

You asked about self-hosting, where data is processed, an NDA and PIPEDA. Here is how the build is set up on each point.

Self-hosting
n8n on a server you own, in a Canadian region

The pipeline runs on your infrastructure, not mine. Your files stay in your Gmail, Slack and Zoho WorkDrive.

AI provider accounts
Your accounts, your settings, your costs

As you said, the AI provider accounts are yours. You control retention settings and see every cost directly.

Where data is processed
Only the minimum per step goes to a model

Each step sends only the text or image it needs. Self-hosted OCR can extract text first without leaving your server. Model processing can stay in a Canadian cloud region where the chosen model is available, confirmed before the build.

My side
Nothing stored on my machines

No client data is stored on my machines. Credentials live in your own secret store, and my access is removed at the end of the engagement.

NDA and data processing agreement
Happy to sign both before I see a single document

Send your NDA and data processing agreement with the go-ahead. Sample documents for testing come after they are signed.

PIPEDA principle
How the build supports it
Consent
The pipeline only processes documents clients sent you for their own mortgage file, for that purpose. No new use of their data.
Limiting collection and use
Only the text or image a step needs is sent to a model. No copies are kept outside your Gmail, Slack and WorkDrive.
Safeguards
Self-hosted on your server, credentials in your secret store, your own AI accounts, a person reviewing every flagged document, my access removed at the end.
Accountability
Every document's path (received, checked, filed, flagged) is visible in Slack and in the n8n run history on your server.

The build is designed to support your PIPEDA obligations. It is not legal advice and not a certification; your privacy officer or counsel confirms the final setup.
